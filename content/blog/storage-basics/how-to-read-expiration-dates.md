**"Best by" means quality, not safety. "Sell by" is for the store, not for you. The only federally regulated date in the U.S. is on infant formula.** Nearly every other food date label is a manufacturer's quality suggestion — and misreading these dates is one of the top causes of unnecessary food waste.

## Key Takeaways

- **Best By / Best Before**: Peak quality — safe to eat past this date
- **Use By**: Manufacturer's last recommended date for peak quality (except infant formula, where it's a safety date)
- **Sell By**: Tells the store when to pull the product — not a consumer safety date
- An estimated **20% of household food waste** comes from date label confusion
- When in doubt: look, smell, and taste before throwing away

## Date Labels Explained

| Label | What It Means | Safety Date? | Can You Eat Past It? |
|-------|---------------|--------------|---------------------|
| Best By / Best Before | Peak quality window | No | Yes — often weeks to months past |
| Use By | Last date for best quality | Only for infant formula | Usually yes for other foods |
| Sell By | Store inventory management | No | Yes — 3-7 days past for most perishables |
| Freeze By | Best date to freeze for quality | No | Yes, if frozen by this date |
| Packed On / Production Date | When it was manufactured | No | Depends on the product |

## Why Are Food Dates So Confusing?

There's no federal standard. The USDA and FDA allow manufacturers to set their own dates using whatever terminology they prefer. A "best by" date on one brand might cover the same timeframe as a "use by" date on another. The lack of standardization leads to wildly different interpretations.

The only exception: **infant formula**. The FDA requires a "use by" date on infant formula because the nutrients degrade over time, and the product may not meet its stated nutritional content after that date. This is the one food date label in the U.S. that is a genuine safety boundary.

## How Long Is Food Safe After the Printed Date?

These are general guidelines for properly stored foods:

### Pantry Items

- **Canned goods** (low-acid like vegetables, meat): 2-5 years past the date
- **Canned goods** (high-acid like tomatoes, fruit): 12-18 months past the date
- **Dried pasta**: 1-2 years past the date
- **Rice** (white): 4-5 years past the date (essentially indefinite if sealed)
- **Cereal**: 6-8 months past the date (opened), 12 months (sealed)
- **Spices**: 1-4 years past the date (lose potency, not safety)

### Refrigerated Items

- **Eggs**: 3-5 weeks past the sell by date
- **Milk**: 5-7 days past the sell by date (trust your nose)
- **Yogurt**: 1-2 weeks past the date
- **Hard cheese**: 3-4 weeks past the date (cut off any mold)
- **Deli meat** (unopened): 5-7 days past the sell by date
- **Deli meat** (opened): 3-5 days regardless of date

### Frozen Items

- **Frozen vegetables**: 8-12 months past the date
- **Frozen fruit**: 8-12 months past the date
- **Frozen meat** (beef, pork): 4-12 months past the date
- **Frozen poultry**: 9-12 months past the date

> **Important:** These timelines assume the food has been stored at proper temperatures. Frozen food kept at a constant 0°F (-18°C) remains safe indefinitely — quality degrades, but safety doesn't.

## How to Tell if Food Has Actually Gone Bad

Dates aside, use your senses:

1. **Look** — Check for mold, discoloration, bloating (in cans or packages), or unusual texture changes
2. **Smell** — Sour, rancid, or "off" odors are a clear sign. This is especially reliable for dairy and meat
3. **Touch** — Slimy texture on meat or produce indicates bacterial growth
4. **Taste** (if it passes the above) — A small taste of something that looks and smells fine will tell you if the flavor has degraded

**When to always discard regardless of the date:**

- Dented, bulging, or rusted cans
- Any food with visible mold (except hard cheese, where you can cut 1 inch around the mold)
- Meat or poultry with a gray-green color and slimy feel
- Anything that smells noticeably "off"

## What's Changing?

Congress has proposed the [Food Date Labeling Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/senate-bill/3159), which would standardize food dates into just two labels:

- **BEST If Used By** — quality date
- **USE By** — safety date (discard after)

If passed, this would eliminate "sell by" and other confusing variations. Several states have already adopted similar frameworks.
