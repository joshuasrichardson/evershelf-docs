**Raw chicken lasts just 1-2 days in the refrigerator, and cooked chicken lasts 3-4 days.** Those numbers come straight from the USDA, and they apply regardless of the sell-by date on the package. Chicken is one of the most popular proteins in the world, but it's also one of the most perishable — and one of the most common sources of foodborne illness when stored incorrectly.

This guide covers every form of chicken you're likely to have in your kitchen: raw and cooked, whole and ground, marinated and frozen. Exact times, clear rules, no guessing.

## Key Takeaways

- **Raw chicken** (whole, breasts, thighs, wings): 1-2 days in the fridge
- **Ground chicken**: 1-2 days in the fridge (same as other raw chicken)
- **Cooked chicken** (all preparations): 3-4 days in the fridge
- **Frozen raw chicken**: 9-12 months (quality starts declining after that)
- **Frozen cooked chicken**: 2-6 months
- Refrigerate chicken within **2 hours** of purchasing or cooking — 1 hour if the temperature is above 90°F (32°C)
- When in doubt, throw it out — chicken-related foodborne illness is not worth the risk

## How Long Does Raw Chicken Last in the Fridge?

According to the USDA, all raw chicken should be cooked or frozen within 1-2 days of refrigeration. This rule applies uniformly across cuts, though there are a few nuances worth knowing.

### Whole Chicken

A whole raw chicken lasts **1-2 days** in the fridge. The cavity of a whole bird can trap moisture and warmth, which encourages bacterial growth. If you buy a whole chicken and don't plan to roast it within two days, break it down into parts and freeze them, or freeze the bird whole.

### Chicken Breasts

Boneless and bone-in chicken breasts last **1-2 days** in the fridge. Boneless, skinless breasts have more exposed surface area, which means bacteria have more room to establish themselves. If you buy the family-size pack from the grocery store, portion and freeze whatever you won't cook within 48 hours.

### Chicken Thighs and Drumsticks

Dark meat cuts — thighs, drumsticks, and leg quarters — follow the same **1-2 day** rule. Bone-in, skin-on pieces have a slight practical advantage because the skin acts as a partial barrier, but not enough to meaningfully extend their fridge life.

### Wings

Raw chicken wings last **1-2 days** in the fridge. Because wings are small, they're often sold in large bags. If you're prepping for a cookout or game day, buy them no more than a day in advance, or thaw frozen wings in the refrigerator overnight.

### Ground Chicken

Ground chicken also lasts **1-2 days** refrigerated. Grinding increases the surface area dramatically, which gives bacteria far more contact points. The USDA considers all ground poultry to be among the most perishable items in your fridge. Treat ground chicken with the same urgency as ground turkey or ground beef — cook it fast or freeze it immediately.

## How Long Does Cooked Chicken Last in the Fridge?

Cooked chicken lasts **3-4 days** in the refrigerator regardless of how it was prepared. The clock starts when the chicken comes off the heat and finishes cooling, not when you put it in the fridge. That said, get it into the refrigerator as quickly as possible — within 2 hours of cooking.

### Roasted or Baked Chicken

A whole roasted chicken or baked chicken pieces last **3-4 days** refrigerated. Remove the meat from the bones before storing if possible. Meat left on the bone cools more slowly and takes up more space in the fridge, both of which work against you.

### Grilled Chicken

Grilled chicken breasts, thighs, or kebabs last **3-4 days**. If you grilled outdoors on a hot day, be especially careful about the 2-hour window — food left out in temperatures above 90°F (32°C) should be refrigerated within 1 hour.

### Fried Chicken

Fried chicken lasts **3-4 days** in the fridge. Store it in a shallow airtight container. The breading will lose its crunch in the fridge, but the chicken itself is safe for the standard window. Reheat in the oven at 375°F (190°C) rather than the microwave to restore some crispness.

### Shredded or Pulled Chicken

Shredded chicken is a meal-prep staple, and it lasts **3-4 days** refrigerated. Because it has so much surface area, store it submerged in a small amount of broth to keep it from drying out. This also makes it easy to reheat.

### Chicken in Soups, Stews, and Casseroles

Chicken-based soups, stews, and casseroles follow the same **3-4 day** rule. The other ingredients in the dish don't extend the chicken's safe storage time. If anything, dairy-based sauces may shorten the window slightly. When in doubt, stick to 3 days.

### Rotisserie Chicken (Store-Bought)

A grocery store rotisserie chicken lasts **3-4 days** from the time you bring it home. The USDA does not give rotisserie chicken special treatment — it's cooked chicken, and the same rules apply. Strip the meat from the bones and refrigerate it within 2 hours of purchase.

## Freezer Times for Chicken

Freezing extends the life of chicken significantly. The USDA notes that food stored at 0°F (-18°C) or below is safe indefinitely, but quality — texture, flavor, moisture — degrades over time. Here are the recommended windows for best quality.

| Chicken Type | Freezer Time (Best Quality) |
|---|---|
| Whole raw chicken | Up to 12 months |
| Raw chicken pieces (breasts, thighs, wings) | Up to 9 months |
| Raw ground chicken | 3-4 months |
| Cooked chicken (plain) | 2-4 months |
| Cooked chicken in broth or gravy | 4-6 months |
| Fried chicken | 4 months |
| Chicken nuggets or patties | 1-3 months |
| Chicken soup or stew | 2-3 months |

### Freezing Tips

- **Wrap tightly.** Use heavy-duty aluminum foil, freezer paper, or freezer-safe zip-top bags. Push out as much air as possible to prevent freezer burn.
- **Label everything.** Write the date, cut, and quantity on each package. A bag of unlabeled frozen chicken from "sometime last year" is the enemy of good meal planning.
- **Freeze in portions.** Freeze chicken breasts individually or in meal-sized portions so you only thaw what you need.
- **Don't refreeze thawed raw chicken** unless you've cooked it first. Refreezing raw chicken that was thawed in the fridge is technically safe according to the USDA, but the quality loss from repeated freeze-thaw cycles makes it not worth it.

## Signs of Spoilage: How to Tell if Chicken Has Gone Bad

Never rely on a single indicator. Check all three — smell, texture, and color — before deciding whether chicken is safe.

### Smell

This is the most reliable test. Fresh raw chicken has a very mild scent or no smell at all. If it smells sour, sulfurous, or like ammonia, it has gone bad. Cooked chicken that has turned will smell distinctly sour or "off" when you open the container. Trust your nose — if something seems wrong, it almost certainly is.

### Texture

Fresh raw chicken feels moist but not slippery. If the surface is slimy or tacky and the sliminess doesn't rinse away under cold water, bacterial colonies have taken hold. Rinsing will not make it safe. Cooked chicken that feels slimy or unusually sticky has also turned.

### Color

Fresh raw chicken is light pink with white fat. As it spoils, the flesh turns gray or takes on a greenish-yellow tint. Cooked chicken that has gone bad may develop dark spots or a dull, grayish appearance. Small color variations (darker thigh meat, slight yellowing on skin) are normal and not cause for concern. Large, obvious shifts — especially toward gray or green — are not.

> **Important:** If your chicken shows any one of these signs, discard it. Do not taste chicken to determine if it's safe. Harmful bacteria like *Salmonella* and *Campylobacter* can be present without obvious spoilage signs, which is why the USDA time guidelines exist as a hard backstop.

## How to Store Chicken Properly

Correct storage is the difference between chicken that lasts the full 2 days (raw) or 4 days (cooked) and chicken that goes bad early.

### Raw Chicken in the Fridge

1. **Keep it in its original packaging** if you'll cook it within 1-2 days. If the packaging is torn or leaking, transfer the chicken to a sealed container or zip-top bag.
2. **Place it on the lowest shelf** of the refrigerator. This prevents raw chicken juices from dripping onto ready-to-eat foods below. Use a plate or rimmed tray underneath as a secondary barrier.
3. **Set your fridge to 40°F (4°C) or below.** Use a refrigerator thermometer to verify — the built-in dial on many fridges is not precise.
4. **Never leave raw chicken on the counter to "come to room temperature."** This old cooking tip is a food safety risk. If you want to take the chill off before cooking, 15-20 minutes on the counter is the maximum the USDA recommends.

### Cooked Chicken in the Fridge

1. **Cool it quickly.** Spread cooked chicken in a shallow container rather than piling it deep. Large, deep containers hold heat in the center and cool too slowly.
2. **Store in airtight containers.** This prevents the chicken from absorbing odors from other foods and slows moisture loss.
3. **Refrigerate within 2 hours of cooking** (1 hour if the ambient temperature is above 90°F). The "danger zone" for bacterial growth is 40-140°F (4-60°C), and bacteria multiply rapidly in that range.
4. **Don't stack hot containers.** Place them where cold air can circulate around them.

### Freezer Storage

1. **Remove chicken from store packaging** if you plan to freeze it for more than 2 months. Store packaging is designed for short-term retail display, not long-term freezing. Rewrap in freezer paper or heavy-duty foil, then place in a freezer bag.
2. **Freeze at 0°F (-18°C) or below.** Check with a freezer thermometer.
3. **Leave a small gap** between packages when initially freezing so cold air can circulate. Once frozen solid, you can stack them tightly.

## Common Questions and Edge Cases

### What if I Left Chicken Out Overnight?

Throw it away. The USDA is unambiguous on this: perishable food left in the danger zone (40-140°F / 4-60°C) for more than 2 hours is unsafe. An entire night on the counter — typically 8+ hours — is well past the point of no return. This applies to both raw and cooked chicken. Cooking the chicken to a high internal temperature will kill active bacteria, but it will not destroy the toxins that some bacteria produce, which can cause illness even after the bacteria themselves are dead.

### How Long Does Thawed Chicken Last?

Chicken thawed in the refrigerator lasts **1-2 days** before it needs to be cooked. The countdown begins once the chicken is fully thawed, not when you move it from the freezer to the fridge.

Chicken thawed in the microwave or in cold water should be cooked **immediately**. These faster thawing methods bring parts of the chicken into the danger zone, so there's no safe window for further refrigerator storage.

### How Long Does Marinated Chicken Last?

Marinated chicken lasts **1-2 days** in the fridge — the same as unmarinated raw chicken. The acid in most marinades (vinegar, citrus juice, yogurt) does not preserve chicken or meaningfully slow bacterial growth at refrigerator temperatures. In fact, poultry should not marinate for more than 24 hours anyway, as extended acid exposure breaks down the protein fibers and turns the texture mushy.

Never reuse marinade that has been in contact with raw chicken unless you bring it to a full rolling boil first.

### Is Chicken Safe After the Sell-By Date?

The sell-by date is a stock-management tool for the grocery store. It is not a safety deadline for consumers. According to the USDA, raw chicken purchased on or before the sell-by date is safe to cook for **1-2 days** after that date, provided it has been properly refrigerated the entire time.

That said, the sell-by date only tells you so much. You have no way of knowing whether the cold chain was maintained perfectly from the processing plant to the store shelf. Always check for spoilage signs when you open the package, regardless of what any date on the label says.

### Can You Refreeze Chicken?

Raw chicken thawed in the refrigerator can technically be refrozen without cooking it first, according to the USDA. However, each freeze-thaw cycle damages the cellular structure of the meat, resulting in drier, tougher chicken. A better approach: cook the thawed chicken first, then freeze the cooked chicken, which gives you a ready-to-eat meal in the freezer.

Chicken thawed via microwave or cold water should **not** be refrozen raw. Cook it first.

### What Temperature Kills Bacteria in Chicken?

All chicken — regardless of cut — should be cooked to an internal temperature of **165°F (74°C)** as measured by a food thermometer inserted into the thickest part. This is the temperature at which *Salmonella*, *Campylobacter*, and other common poultry pathogens are destroyed. There is no safe "medium rare" for chicken.
